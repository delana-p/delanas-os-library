# Multi-Year Cleanup — Construction Company

## Business Profile

A general contractor, specialty contractor, or construction company managing multiple customers and jobs with progress billing, subcontractors, materials, labor, equipment, retainage, deposits, and project-related cash requirements.

## Common Historical Issues

- Customers, sub-customers, and projects used inconsistently
- Job revenue and costs posted without project attribution
- Customer deposits, progress billings, and earned revenue combined
- Retainage receivable or payable omitted
- WIP and overbilling or underbilling schedules missing or unsupported
- Change orders and unbilled work missing from project reporting
- Labor, subcontractor, material, equipment, and overhead costs misclassified
- Vendor and subcontractor balances unsupported
- Loans, equipment financing, credit lines, and fixed assets not reconciled
- Payroll liabilities and contractor reporting incomplete
- Closed jobs with unresolved balances
- Financial statements that do not agree with job-level activity

## Cleanup Workflow

### Job and Customer Structure

Rebuild the customer and project structure needed to organize historical contracts, invoices, receipts, costs, and project balances.

### Revenue and Billing Review

Review contracts, progress invoices, deposits, retainage, change orders, credits, collections, and open receivables.

### Job-Cost Reconstruction

Organize labor, subcontractors, materials, equipment, permits, freight, and other direct costs by project when sufficient support is available.

### Vendor, Payroll, and Liability Review

Reconcile vendor balances, subcontractor activity, payroll liabilities, credit cards, loans, credit lines, and equipment financing.

### Project and Balance-Sheet Review

Identify unresolved project balances, unbilled activity, customer deposits, retainage, fixed assets, and other construction-specific balance-sheet items.

### Year-by-Year Close

Complete historical corrections and reconciliations in sequence, confirm year-end rollforwards, and align financial statements with supported project activity.

## Final Deliverables

- Corrected financial statements by year
- Customer and project cleanup summary
- Job-revenue and job-cost review
- AR, deposits, progress-billing, retainage, and WIP schedules where supported
- Vendor, subcontractor, payroll, loan, and equipment schedules
- Project-level open-items report
- Cleanup adjustment summary
- Recommended construction accounting and monthly close structure
