# Accounting Control Framework

## Revenue and Settlement Controls

- Preserve sales, taxes, discounts, refunds, fees, reserves, and chargebacks separately.
- Reconcile expected settlement to actual settlement and bank activity.
- Maintain clearing accounts by channel or processor where appropriate.
- Roll supported settlement-in-transit balances forward without duplicating revenue.
- Quantify unexplained variance separately from supported uncleared balances.

## Accounts Receivable Controls

- Match customer invoices to remittance and cash.
- Preserve open AR for short pays and unsupported deductions.
- Keep unapplied cash visible until sufficient matching evidence exists.
- Validate credits, returns, allowances, and chargebacks before application.
- Review stale receivables, duplicate invoices, and cross-period activity.

## High-Volume Controls

- Tie accounting batches to transaction-level source control totals.
- Preserve the link from summarized accounting entries to source activity.
- Detect missing, duplicate, or out-of-period transactions.
- Stop or quarantine batches that fail completeness controls.

## Inventory and COGS Controls

- Reconcile beginning inventory, receipts, returns, approved adjustments, outbound movement, and ending inventory.
- Reconcile controlled 3PL inventory schedules to supported QBO balances.
- Separate in-transit, damaged, returned, and shrink activity.
- Attribute outbound product cost to the correct customer or channel.
- Review timing and cutoff independently from settlement timing.

## Fulfillment and Margin Controls

- Separate storage, pick-and-pack, freight, accessorials, and other 3PL charges.
- Compare 3PL billing to approved rate structures when support is available.
- Preserve channel/customer attribution for contribution reporting.
- Treat unsupported cost allocation as an exception, not a completed margin result.

## Required Human Review

Human approval is required for material:

- Write-offs and credits
- Retailer deductions and chargebacks
- Manual journal entries
- Mapping or threshold changes
- Inventory adjustments, shrinkage, and write-downs
- Unusual revenue, COGS, freight, or fulfillment classification
- Accounting-policy decisions
- Exceptions that cannot be supported from available evidence

## Close Standard

A workflow is not considered closed merely because QBO reconciles. The close package should also demonstrate source completeness, channel-level tie-outs, supported rollforwards, separately quantified variance, resolved or approved exceptions, and retained audit evidence.
